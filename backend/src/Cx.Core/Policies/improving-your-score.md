# Improving Your Customer Satisfaction Score

## Focus on the two levers you control
A dealer score is the average of the recommend score and the vehicle condition score. To raise the score you must do two things:
make customers happy enough to answer "Highly recommend", and make sure every customer receives a car with no missing part and no defect.
Compare your average recommend score with your average vehicle condition score on the dashboard to see which lever is costing you more points.

## Make every customer happy
Only "Highly recommend" earns 100 points on the recommend question. "Recommend" earns 50 and "Might recommend" earns 25.
To move customers up to "Highly recommend":
- Keep the customer informed at every step of the purchase and delivery, and never let a promised date slip without telling them first.
- Make the delivery handover a personal event: explain the features, pair the phone, and answer questions without rushing.
- Call the customer within 48 hours of delivery to check that everything is working and they are happy.
- Introduce the customer to the service team so they know who to contact.

## Resolve issues before the customer fills in the survey
The survey is most damaging when a problem is still unresolved at the time the customer answers it.
Before a survey invitation is sent, check that the customer has no open issue: a pending repair, a missing accessory that is on backorder, or an unanswered complaint.
Resolve the issue first, tell the customer it is resolved, and only then let the survey go out. A customer whose problem was fixed quickly is often happier than one who never had a problem.
If a problem cannot be fixed before the survey, contact the customer personally, explain the plan and give a firm date.

## Eliminate defects and missing parts
The vehicle condition question is the easiest place to gain points because the dealer controls it directly.
A single missing floor mat or a scratch costs 50 points on that survey, which lowers the net score of that survey by 25 points.
Use a pre-delivery inspection checklist for every vehicle, confirm all accessories and keys are present, and photograph the car at handover.
See the defects and missing parts guidance for the full inspection process.

## Get more customers to fill in the survey
A larger number of completed surveys makes your score more stable and more representative. Scores built on very few surveys swing widely because a single unhappy customer has a large effect.
Encourage every customer to respond: tell them at delivery that the survey is coming, explain that their feedback is valued, and follow up politely if they have not answered.
Never coach customers on how to answer, offer rewards for good scores, or complete surveys on behalf of a customer; this is a policy violation.

## Recover unhappy customers
When a customer is not happy, call them personally within one business day. Listen first, apologise sincerely, agree the fix and a date, and follow up when it is done.
Review every survey with a net score below 50 in your weekly team meeting, identify the root cause, and record the corrective action.

## A simple improvement plan
1. Identify the weaker lever: the average recommend score or the average vehicle condition score.
2. Fix the process behind it: delivery experience for recommend, pre-delivery inspection for condition.
3. Resolve open issues before surveys go out.
4. Review low scoring surveys weekly.
5. Track your score and rank on the dashboard and compare with the previous period.
