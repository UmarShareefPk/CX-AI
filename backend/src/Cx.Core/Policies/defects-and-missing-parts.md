# Defects and Missing Parts

## Definitions
A missing part is anything that should have been delivered with the new car and was not: floor mats, spare key, wheel lock key, owner's manual, cargo cover, or an ordered accessory.
A defect is a fault in the vehicle at delivery: cosmetic damage such as scratches or dents, a warning light, an interior trim fault, a malfunctioning feature, or a fluid leak.
Customers report these in Question 2 of the survey: "Part missing", "Defect", or "Part missing and defect".

## Score impact
A report of a missing part or a defect scores 50 points on Question 2, and a report of both scores 0 points.
Because the net score is the average of both questions, one defect report lowers the net score of that survey by 25 points, and a report of both lowers it by 50 points, compared with an all-good delivery.
A customer who reports a problem also tends to answer Question 1 less favourably, so a defect usually costs more than the 25 points on Question 2 alone.

## Pre-delivery inspection checklist
Every new vehicle must pass a pre-delivery inspection (PDI) before it is handed to the customer:
- Compare the vehicle with the sales order and confirm every ordered accessory is fitted or in the box.
- Confirm the number of keys, the key fob batteries, the wheel lock key, floor mats, manual and any cargo accessories.
- Walk around the exterior in good light and record every scratch, dent or paint fault. Repair before delivery.
- Check the interior trim, seats and screens, and test every switch, light, camera and infotainment function.
- Start the vehicle, check for warning lights, and check fluid levels and tyre pressures.
- Sign the checklist. The technician and the sales manager must both sign before delivery.

## Delivery handover
Walk the customer around the car at handover and let them confirm the condition. Photograph the vehicle with the customer present.
If a part is on backorder or a minor repair is needed, tell the customer before delivery, give a firm date, and put it in writing so it is not a surprise later.

## When a problem is found after delivery
Contact the customer within one business day. Arrange the repair or replacement at the customer's convenience, offer a courtesy vehicle when the car is off the road, and confirm with the customer when the work is done.
Resolve the issue before the survey invitation is sent wherever possible, because an unresolved defect is the single biggest cause of low survey scores.
Record every defect and missing part in the delivery quality log and review the log monthly to find repeat causes, such as a particular accessory supplier or a specific inspection step being skipped.
